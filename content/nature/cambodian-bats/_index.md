Eighty bat species are now confirmed in Cambodia, and the list keeps growing as
surveys reach new places.{{< cite "checklist" >}} They range from large fruit bats
to small insect-eaters that most people never notice.

![A fruit bat hanging in a tree](/images/nature/cambodian-bats/fruit-bat.jpg)
*A fruit bat roosting. Photo: rigel / Unsplash.*

## Why they matter

Lyle's flying fox, one of the country's best-known bats, feeds on fruit, flowers,
nectar and pollen, and spreads seeds as it goes.{{< cite "lyle" >}} It lives in
Cambodia, Thailand, Vietnam and Yunnan in China.{{< cite "lyle" >}}

## Under pressure

Lyle's flying fox is listed as Vulnerable, and its numbers have fallen by more than
30% in fifteen years.{{< cite "lyle" >}} For many other species, too little is known
to say how they are doing at all.

## What we do

Bats are far easier to record than to see. Our [bat call classification](/projects/bat_calls_classification/)
work teaches models to recognise species from their echolocation calls, so the
recordings Cambodia's citizen scientists collect can become maps of where each
species lives.
