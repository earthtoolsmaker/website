Mountains cover 24% of Earth's land, and more than half of humanity depends on them
for water.{{< cite "wiki" >}} Climb high enough and the world changes every few
hundred metres, with plants and animals stacked in bands, each adapted to its own
slice of altitude.{{< cite "wiki" >}}

## Why they matter

High mountains are where the large carnivores still have room. Snow leopards hunt
the alpine zones of Central Asia between 3,000 and 4,500 m,{{< cite "leopard" >}}
and the Carpathians, 1,500 km long, hold Europe's largest populations of brown
bears, wolves and lynx.{{< cite "carp" >}}

## Under pressure

Mountain environments are especially sensitive to climate change and are changing
faster than at any time in the last 10,000 years, with glaciers shrinking and
landslides becoming more frequent.{{< cite "wiki" >}}

Our [snow leopard monitoring](/projects/snow_leopard_monitoring/) recognises
