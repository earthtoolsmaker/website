A coral reef is built by colonies of tiny animals, coral polyps, held together by the
calcium carbonate they lay down.{{< cite "wiki" >}} Inside the polyps live microscopic
algae, zooxanthellae, that provide the coral with nutrients.{{< cite "wiki" >}} Together
they have built structures that cover less than 0.1% of the ocean yet shelter at
least a quarter of all marine species.{{< cite "wiki" >}}

## Why they matter

Reefs support fisheries and tourism and protect shorelines, services estimated to be
worth billions of dollars a year.{{< cite "wiki" >}}

## Under pressure

Rising ocean heat, acidification, overfishing and nutrient pollution are all taking a
toll,{{< cite "wiki" >}} and shallow tropical reefs have declined by half since
1950.{{< cite "wiki" >}} A coral that loses its algae turns white, or bleaches, and
can die unless conditions recover in time.{{< cite "wiki" >}}

## What we do

Knowing whether a reef is recovering means measuring coral cover, survey after
survey. Our [coral reef work](/projects/coral_reef_health_monitoring/) segments every
coral colony in underwater photos and separates hard from soft coral, turning hours
of manual annotation into a repeatable measurement.
