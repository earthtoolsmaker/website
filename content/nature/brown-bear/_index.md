Brown bears are native to Eurasia and North America and live mainly in forests, from
lowlands up to 5,000 m.{{< cite "wiki" >}} Around 200,000 remain, with the largest
populations in Russia, the United States and Canada.{{< cite "wiki" >}} The species
is listed as Least Concern worldwide,{{< cite "iucn" >}} but it has disappeared from
parts of its range and declined sharply in others.{{< cite "wiki" >}}

## Why it matters

Where salmon run, bears are part of how the ocean feeds the forest. They carry fish
away from the water and leave the rest to foxes, eagles, ravens and gulls, and the
nitrogen in those remains reaches the trees: up to 24% of the nitrogen in riverside
woodland has been traced to salmon.{{< cite "salmon" >}}

## Under pressure

In Europe, the Carpathians hold the largest populations of brown bears, wolves and
lynx, with the highest concentration in Romania.{{< cite "carp" >}} Bears and people
there share the same valleys, and encounters around villages and farms are where
conflict, for both, begins.

## What we do

In British Columbia, our [bear identification](/projects/bear_identification/) work
recognises individual bears from their faces in camera-trap photos. In the
Carpathians, our [bear deterrence](/projects/carpathian-bear-deterrence/) project
spots bears at the edge of Romanian farms and scares them off, harmlessly, before
conflict starts.
