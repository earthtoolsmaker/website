Harbour seals live along the coasts of the northern Atlantic and Pacific, including
the Baltic and North seas, with an estimated 350,000 to 500,000 worldwide.{{< cite "wiki" >}}
They return to familiar resting spots to haul out, on rocks, ice, sand or
mud.{{< cite "wiki" >}} The species is listed as Least Concern.{{< cite "iucn" >}}

## Why it matters

As top-level feeders in kelp forests, harbour seals enhance species diversity and
productivity.{{< cite "wiki" >}} In the Wadden Sea, 8,230 pups and
23,772 moulting seals were counted in 2024, figures that only mean something when
they are repeated, year after year.{{< cite "whs" >}}

## What we do

Counting and describing thousands of seals by eye from aerial photos takes hundreds
of hours every season. Our [Wadden Sea seal monitoring](/projects/wadden_sea_seal_monitoring/)
work detects each seal in the survey photos and records its species, life stage and
more, and can recognise individuals from their whisker and face patterns.
