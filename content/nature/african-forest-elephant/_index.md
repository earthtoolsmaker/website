The African forest elephant lives in the humid tropical forests of West Africa and
the Congo Basin, and is considerably smaller than its savanna cousin.{{< cite "wiki" >}}
Numbers have fallen from around 700,000 to fewer than 100,000 over several
decades,{{< cite "wiki" >}} and in 2021 the species was listed as Critically
Endangered.{{< cite "iucn" >}} Gabon
holds the largest stable population.{{< cite "wiki" >}}

## Why it matters

Forest elephants are the gardeners of the rainforest. They swallow large fruit and
carry the seeds more than 5 km before dropping them, and 14 of 18 megafaunal tree
species studied depend on them to spread.{{< cite "wiki" >}} Protecting them also
helps the forest keep storing carbon.{{< cite "wiki" >}}

## Under pressure

Poaching for ivory and bushmeat is a major threat across Central Africa, alongside
forest cleared for plantations and settlements.{{< cite "wiki" >}}

## What we do

In dense forest, elephants are easier to hear than to see: they keep in touch with
low-frequency rumbles.{{< cite "wiki" >}} Our [acoustic monitoring](/projects/elephants_passive_acoustic_monitoring/)
work finds those rumbles in months of recordings from forest microphones, so
researchers can follow where elephants move without ever seeing one.
