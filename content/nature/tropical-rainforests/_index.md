The Congolian rainforests are the world's second-largest tropical forest after the
Amazon, covering about 2 million square kilometres across seven countries, from
Cameroon and Gabon to the Democratic Republic of the Congo.{{< cite "wiki" >}} They
hold a quarter of the world's remaining tropical forest.{{< cite "wiki" >}}

## Why they matter

More than 10,000 plant species grow here, 30% of them found nowhere else, alongside
over 400 mammals, 1,000 birds and 700 fish, including gorillas, chimpanzees, bonobos
and African forest elephants.{{< cite "wiki" >}}

## Under pressure

Commercial logging, oil palm plantations and mining are cutting up the forest, and
the bushmeat trade and poaching are emptying it of wildlife.{{< cite "wiki" >}}
Deforestation doubled between 2015 and 2019.{{< cite "wiki" >}}

## What we do

In dense forest, animals are far easier to hear than to see. Our
[acoustic monitoring](/projects/elephants_passive_acoustic_monitoring/) work listens
for forest elephant rumbles, and for gunshots, across months of recordings, to show
where elephants move and where they are at risk.
