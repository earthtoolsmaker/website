The westslope cutthroat trout lives in cold, clean headwater lakes and streams of the
upper Missouri and Columbia basins, in British Columbia, Alberta, Montana and
Idaho.{{< cite "wiki" >}} It takes its name from the red, pink or orange mark beneath
the jaw, which also tells it apart from rainbow trout.{{< cite "wiki" >}}

![A westslope cutthroat trout: golden body, black spots and the red slash beneath the jaw](/images/projects/trout_identification/wct_example.png)

## Under pressure

Genetically pure westslope cutthroat trout now survive in less than 3% of their
historic range.{{< cite "wiki" >}} Introduced rainbow trout interbreed with them,
logging and dams degrade their streams, and invasive lake trout and kokanee compete
for food.{{< cite "wiki" >}} The species is considered threatened in its native range
in Alberta.{{< cite "wiki" >}}

## What we do

Every cutthroat carries its own pattern of black spots. In the Elk River, British
Columbia, our [trout identification](/projects/trout_identification/) work matches
those patterns between photos, so biologists can recognise a fish they have met
before without tagging it.
