Snow leopards live on the cliffs and ridges of Central Asia's high mountains, in
alpine zones between 3,000 and 4,500 m.{{< cite "wiki" >}} They are solitary, most
active from dawn into the early morning, and use their long, flexible tail both for
balance on broken rock and as a blanket over the face while they sleep.{{< cite "wiki" >}}
Fewer than 10,000 mature adults remain,{{< cite "wiki" >}} and the species is listed
as Vulnerable.{{< cite "iucn" >}}

## Why it matters

The snow leopard hunts blue sheep, Himalayan tahr, argali, markhor and wild goats,
and can take most of the hoofed animals that share its range.{{< cite "wiki" >}} A
healthy population means a mountain that still has room, and prey, for a large cat.

## Under pressure

Poaching for skins and body parts, the loss of wild prey to overgrazing and hunting,
and killings in defence of livestock are the main drivers of its decline.{{< cite "wiki" >}}
Warming is expected to push the treeline up the Himalaya and shrink the alpine zone
it depends on.{{< cite "wiki" >}}

## What we do

No two snow leopards share a coat pattern. Our [snow leopard monitoring](/projects/snow_leopard_monitoring/)
work matches those patterns across camera-trap photos, so a survey counts individual
cats rather than pictures of them, without ever needing to catch one.
